How this was made, and how to check it

Everything on the main page traces to a public document. This page says who made the analysis and how, lists every figure with its source, and records what was wrong in the first version.

Disclosure

  • Author. Matthew R. V. Ross, acting as an individual. This is not a publication of Colorado State University or the CSU System, and no one at the university reviewed it.
  • Use of AI. AI coding agents did most of the work. The first version (November 19–20, 2026) was built with Google's Gemini in the Antigravity agent: it downloaded the source documents, wrote the code that reads them, and drafted the pages. On November 20, 2026, Anthropic's Claude re-read the source documents, checked each claim against them, searched news coverage for facts not in the archive, removed what could not be supported, and rewrote the site. The author directed both passes and is responsible for what is published.
  • What "checked" means. For each figure, the AI located the number in the source document (or computed it from numbers located there) and recorded the page. Budget slides that exist only as images were read from the rendered image. A person has not independently re-keyed every figure. AI systems make transcription and reading errors; the ledger below exists so that anyone can check any number in a minute.
  • Limits. The analysis uses only public documents. CSU has internal data that could change the picture. Election results are unofficial until certified. Facts sourced to news reports rely on those outlets' reporting.
  • No recommendation. The site describes the problem. It does not propose or endorse any solution.
  • Corrections. Report errors at https://github.com/matthewross07/csu_budget_context/issues.

What the re-audit changed

The first version of this site contained errors and unsupported claims. They are listed here so readers of that version know what not to rely on.

  1. The claim that no number came from AI memory was not true. The first version said every figure was computed from archived filings. Several statements had no archived source at all, including contract details for the football coach, terms of the president's departure, and the election results. Those that could be confirmed are now cited to news reports and labeled as such. Those that could not were removed.
  2. A paragraph about a named private individual was removed. The first version included a 'record check' asserting that a named Title IX consultant had no connection to CSU. Nothing in the archive supported the paragraph in either direction, and it is not the kind of claim to publish about a person without evidence.
  3. The FY28 gap was presented as a three-part breakdown that CSU never published. CSU has published the total ($61.3M-$70.7M) and the enrollment figure (about $25M). The first version showed a third bar, 'mandatory costs, compensation and state squeeze' of $21.2M-$30.6M, as if it were a finding. It was the remainder after subtracting the other two. Whether the $15.1M of expiring one-time money sits inside CSU's total is an inference. The slides that held CSU's breakdown (slides 14-15 of the Oct. 14 deck) are blank in the posted PDF. This version labels each piece accordingly.
  4. Amendment 87 was described as relieving pressure on higher education funding. The first version said the amendment would absorb K-12 and Medicaid costs and estimated $6.3M-$10.6M more per year for CSU. The state's ballot booklet says the new revenue must add to, and not replace, spending on K-12, health care and early childhood. The estimate was speculation and has been removed.
  5. A pie chart assigned percentage 'weights' to causes with no calculation behind them. The '45% payroll, 22% debt, 17% athletics, 16% state' chart was labeled illustrative, but the numbers were invented. It has been removed.
  6. Athletics support was understated, then used to size the remaining gap. The first version netted athletes' scholarships out of university support to reach '$15.6M net' and concluded that 76% of the gap would remain without athletics. Whether to net out scholarships is a judgment, not a fact. This version reports the figures as filed: $26.0M of direct and indirect support, $6.1M of student fees, and a $17.2M general-fund allocation. The state auditor's report is also an agreed-upon-procedures engagement on an unaudited statement, not an audit, and is now described that way.
  7. The staffing headline used the one data source that showed the least growth. 'Workforce grew 7.0%, matching enrollment at 6.6%' came from the state filing. CSU's Fact Book shows general-fund faculty up 13.0% and other staff up 19.6% over nearly the same period. The first version also compared staff growth measured from FY15 with faculty growth measured from FY17. Both counts are now shown side by side with the same base year, and the disagreement is stated.
  8. The '$885M E&G budget' includes units with their own appropriations. That total includes the veterinary program, the Agricultural Experiment Station, Extension and the Forest Service. The general campus budget is about $700M. As a result the first version overstated the state's share of the budget where the gap sits (23% rather than 16%) and the value of a 1% state increase to that budget ($2.1M rather than about $1.1M).
  9. The October 7 budget message was attributed to the president. It was signed by the Vice President for University Operations and Chief Financial Officer.
  10. Comparisons that rested on weak or unverified sources were removed. Athletics debt medians attributed to a federal GAO report came from a third-party blog summary. NCAA tournament payout scenarios used unsourced assumptions. Peer comparisons from federal IPEDS data were not re-verified in this audit. None of these is needed for the argument, so they are gone.
  11. Two international-enrollment figures from different slides were mixed. CSU's October 14 deck gives different numbers on slides 5 and 6. This version uses slide 5 and says so.
  12. Data-parsing errors in the first version's intermediate files. For example, the FY15 College Opportunity Fund stipend was read as $15 and FY15 average faculty compensation as $206,154. These did not reach the headline numbers, but the series used here were re-extracted and checked row by row.
