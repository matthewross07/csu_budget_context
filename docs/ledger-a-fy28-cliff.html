Ledger A: Immediate Drivers of the FY28 Operating Gap

Enrollment Mix Shifts, Expiring FY27 One-Time Bridges, and Mandatory Cost Increases Under Flat State Support

Before weighing long-term structural reforms, it helps to examine what creates the immediate $61.3M–$70.7M ($66.0M midpoint) gap projected for FY28. Every figure on this page draws directly from CSU’s Oct. 7, 2026 campus budget update, Oct. 14, 2026 Fall Budget Forum census slides, and May 13, 2026 Campus V4 FY27 budget materials.

1. Enrollment Shifts & Higher-Margin Non-Resident Mix (~$25.00M)

CSU’s Fall 2026 census highlights an important nuance: campus welcomed a record incoming class of Colorado resident students and raised its 4-year graduation rate from 51.1% to 55.1%, even as total Resident Instruction (RI) tuition revenue declined.

Two overlapping trends explain that result: 1. Faster Degree Completion: Graduating more students in four years (51.1% → 55.1%) is a clear academic success, while mechanically reducing the pool of continuing 5th-year tuition-paying undergraduates. 2. Non-Resident & International Contraction: Non-resident students pay roughly triple in-state rates and contributed 48.7% of CSU’s Format 30 E&G tuition ($268.9M in FY26) on 33.1% of student FTE. In Fall 2026, Domestic Non-Resident Undergraduates declined -9.7% (-685 students), International Undergraduates fell -6.7%, and International Graduate enrollment fell -13.9%, yielding an estimated ~$25.00M revenue impact (Oct. 7 Memo; Oct. 14 Slides 4–6).

2. Expiring FY27 One-Time Bridge Funds ($15.11M)

Examining how CSU balanced FY27’s $54.272M V4 budget shortfall (Campus-V4-5-13-2026_a-1.pdf, Slides 5–6) clarifies why FY28 starts with a recurring carry-forward gap:

  • FY27 new expenses totaled $61.103M (including $33.665M for the 3% faculty/AP/GA compensation pool, state classified mandates, and fringe benefits; $8.826M multi-year strategic investments; $8.120M academic incentives; and $8.678M mandatory cost escalation).
  • Net new resources provided $6.831M ($11.860M net rate/enrollment impact minus -$5.029M institutional aid offset), leaving -$54.272M to resolve.
  • Campus closed $39.160M via ongoing adjustments ($7.406M central savings, $28.754M campus unit reductions, and $3.000M vacant line reductions) and bridged the remaining $15.112M with one-time savings that expire on June 30, 2027.

3. Mandatory Cost Escalation, Compensation & State Budget Context ($21.19M–$30.59M)

Under the Board of Governors’ three initial FY28 scenarios (modeling 0%, 1%, and 3% state funding increases alongside matching compensation scenarios), health insurance, utilities, classified step mandates, and salary pools on a $900.98M State Appropriated / E&G base add $21.19M to $30.59M net of anticipated state support.

Meanwhile, Colorado’s September 2026 OSPB economic forecast projects a state general-fund shortfall of up to $1.5B in FY28, largely absorbed by Medicaid obligations (Oct. 7 Memo), keeping CSU’s state Fee-for-Service and COF support essentially flat at $211.05M (23.4% of State Appropriated / E&G revenue in FY27).

Where FY27’s 160.58 Permanent FTE Reductions Occurred

May 13 V4 Forum disclosures (Slides 7–8) also clarify how FY27 reductions were distributed across campus: 160.58 permanent FTE were eliminated across 30 units, comprising 91.84 Admin Pro FTE (57.2%), 28.60 State Classified (17.8%), 22.62 Non-TTF (14.1%), and 17.52 TTF (10.9%). Academic colleges and administrative/athletics support divisions both participated in those reductions (Liberal Arts 18.59 FTE, CVMBS & PVM 14.85 FTE, Engineering 14.17 FTE, alongside VP University Operations 10.35 FTE, VP Marketing & Comms 7.20 FTE, and Athletics 4.40 AP FTE).

Ledger A Takeaway: Even holding one-time executive settlements and coaching contracts at $0, FY28’s enrollment mix impact (~$25.0M), expiring FY27 one-time bridge funds ($15.11M), and mandatory compensation/utility escalation ($21.19M–$30.59M) account for virtually the entire $61.3M–$70.7M incremental projection.
